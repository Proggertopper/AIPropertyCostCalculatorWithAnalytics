How to use the Property Sale hub

A home sale isn’t just sale price minus what you paid. Your net proceeds depend on agent commissions, transfer taxes and escrow/title fees (varies by state), seller credits, repairs, and your mortgage payoff. Small differences in costs can move your net by thousands.

Start with the calculator, then use the guides to pick a selling strategy (agent vs FSBO, as-is vs renovated), set a realistic list price, and prepare for inspections, buyer requests, and closing timelines.

Property Sale FAQ

How do I sell a house?

The usual flow is: prep + pricing → photos/listing → showings → offers → contract → inspection/appraisal → final closing. Most surprises come from inspections, buyer concessions, and closing costs.

How do I calculate net proceeds when selling?

Net proceeds are roughly: sale price − agent fees − seller closing costs − concessions/repairs − mortgage payoff (and liens, if any). A “net sheet” estimate helps you compare offers correctly.

What closing costs does the seller pay?

It depends on state and contract. Sellers often pay agent commissions and may pay some title/escrow, transfer taxes, and buyer credits. Many items are negotiable depending on market conditions.

Should I sell my house as-is?

As-is can make sense if repairs are large, timeline is tight, or you want fewer projects. The tradeoff is usually a lower price or stronger buyer negotiations. A clean, safe, “showable” home still helps.

Do I pay capital gains tax when I sell my home?

Sometimes. Many primary-residence sellers can qualify for exclusions, but taxes depend on your gain, filing status, how long you lived there, and other factors. It’s worth modeling before you list.
