How to use the Mortgage Calculator hub

This hub helps you understand the real cost of a mortgage using a simple framework: monthly payment (principal + interest), total interest, and the amortization schedule (how much of each payment goes to interest vs principal over time). The guides below explain the inputs that change your payment the most: interest rate, term length (15 vs 30 years), down payment, points, PMI, and extra payments.

If you’re unsure where to start, read How monthly payments are calculated first, then open the mortgage calculator and test two scenarios: (1) your base rate and (2) a higher-rate scenario to see how sensitive your payment and total interest are.

Mortgage Calculator FAQ

What does a mortgage calculator include?

Most calculators include principal + interest (P&I). Some also estimate taxes and insurance (escrow). For the most accurate monthly number, include your local property tax and insurance estimates, plus PMI if your down payment is under 20%.

Why is my payment so sensitive to interest rate?

A mortgage is a long-duration loan. Small rate changes compound over 30 years and can move the payment a lot, especially at higher prices. Always test a +1% scenario.

Is it better to do 15-year or 30-year?

A 15-year loan saves interest and builds equity faster, but the payment is higher and reduces flexibility. Many people choose a 30-year for flexibility and optionally make extra payments.

Do extra payments always make sense?

Extra payments reduce interest and shorten the loan, but the trade-off is opportunity cost—what that money could earn if invested. Compare both strategies using realistic assumptions.
