How to use the Homeownership Cost hub

The true cost of owning a home is your mortgage payment plus operating costs and long-term capital expenses. Some costs are predictable (taxes, insurance, HOA), while others are lumpy (roof, HVAC, plumbing). A good model separates monthly expenses from “CapEx” reserves for big repairs.

Homeownership Cost FAQ

What is the true cost of homeownership?

The true cost includes more than the mortgage: property taxes, insurance, HOA (if any), utilities, maintenance, repairs/CapEx reserves, and transaction costs over time.

How much should I budget for maintenance each year?

Many homeowners budget a percentage of home value or use system-based planning (roof, HVAC, etc.). The right number depends on home age, condition, climate, and how much you DIY.

What are the hidden costs of buying a house?

Common “hidden” costs include closing costs, immediate repairs, tools and equipment, landscaping, higher utilities, HOA/special assessments, and tax/insurance increases after reassessment or renewal.

Why did my monthly housing cost increase after I bought?

Taxes and insurance can rise, and escrow can be recalculated, increasing your payment. New owners also often see higher first-year repair and setup costs.

Is owning always cheaper than renting?

Not always. Owning can be more expensive monthly but builds equity and has different risk/benefits. A good comparison uses all-in costs plus time horizon, opportunity cost, and local rent vs price.
