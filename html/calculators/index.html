How to choose the right calculator

Pick the calculator by decision, not by formula. Start with the question you need to answer, then use a more detailed tool for validation.

  • Buying vs renting: start with Rent vs Buy for total cost comparison over time.
  • Monthly affordability: use Mortgage to estimate payment, interest, and term impact.
  • Investment performance: begin with Cash Flow, then confirm return quality in IRR.
  • Tax and ownership burden: review Property Tax and Homeownership Cost.

What to prepare before calculating

Results are only as good as inputs. A short prep saves rework and gives more realistic scenarios.

  • Purchase price, down payment, rate, and loan term.
  • Property tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, and vacancy assumptions.
  • Rent estimate and expected annual growth for income scenarios.
  • A base case and a stress case so you can compare downside risk.

Quick FAQ

Which calculator should beginners use first?

Start with Rent vs Buy for decisions about living, or Mortgage if your main question is monthly payment capacity.

How many scenarios should I run?

At minimum two: base and stress. If both look acceptable, the decision is usually more robust.

Can one calculator replace all others?

No. Use one for the first estimate, then validate with a return-focused and risk-focused calculator.
