How to use the Property Tax hub

A property tax bill is usually based on assessed value × tax rate, plus local rules, exemptions, and fees. “Tax rate” may be expressed as a percentage or as a millage rate. Because assessment systems differ by state/county, two similar homes can have very different bills.

Property Tax FAQ

How do I calculate property tax?

In most areas, property tax is based on: assessed value × tax rate, then reduced by exemptions (if any). Your local jurisdiction may express the rate as a percentage or as a millage rate.

What’s the difference between assessed value and market value?

Market value is what buyers may pay. Assessed value is a taxable value set by the assessor using local rules and cycles. They often differ, and reassessment can occur after purchase, new construction, or major improvements.

What is a millage rate?

Millage is “tax per $1,000 of value.” For example, 20 mills means $20 per $1,000 of taxable value (before exemptions). Convert mills to an effective rate by dividing by 1,000.

Can I lower my property taxes?

Sometimes. The biggest levers are claiming exemptions you qualify for (like homestead) and appealing an assessment if it’s higher than reasonable comps or if property details are incorrect.

Why did my mortgage payment go up because of property tax?

If you escrow taxes, your lender estimates the next bill and adjusts your monthly payment. If taxes rise or the estimate was low, you can get an escrow shortage that increases payments until it’s covered.
